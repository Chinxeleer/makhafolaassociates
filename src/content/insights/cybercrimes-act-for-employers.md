The Cybercrimes Act 19 of 2020 created specific offences for conduct such as unlawful access to computer systems, interception of data, cyber fraud and the sharing of harmful messages. Several parts of it touch the workplace directly.

## Offences that can happen inside a business

An employee who accesses systems beyond their authority, copies data they are not entitled to, or interferes with company data may commit an offence under the Act. Clear access policies help in two ways: they reduce the risk, and they make it possible to show what access was authorised.

## Harmful messages

The Act makes it an offence to send certain harmful data messages, including messages that incite violence or that share intimate images without consent. Employers should make sure their workplace policies address this conduct on company systems.

## Reporting duties

Electronic communications service providers and financial institutions must report certain offences to the South African Police Service within a set time once they become aware of them, and must preserve related information.

## Practical steps

- Review who has access to which systems, and remove access that is no longer needed.
- Update IT and acceptable-use policies to refer to the Act.
- Decide in advance who handles a suspected incident, and how evidence is preserved.
